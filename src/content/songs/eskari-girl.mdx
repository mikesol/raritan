Maija was five, maybe six, and in her last year of *eskari* -- Finnish preschool, the year before first grade. This song wrote itself. Every line is just... her. The Eskari dolls, the Eskari schemes, the dodgeball readiness, the "dad guy don't you stay." All real.

It's a Barbie Girl parody, obviously, but the structure only holds for the first half. Then the song breaks free of the joke and turns into what it actually is: a dad watching his daughter become a person, in real time, and trying not to lose it.

The rapid-fire couplet section near the end -- "My oh my / She don't cry / Flying high" -- that's the part that gets me. It's the part where the song stops being clever and starts being true.
