In college, I played in a band called Two Mikes from Jersey. It was a wild ride, we went from playing the occasional set at the coffee house to nearly falling apart our junior year to a raging comeback as (mostly) seniors.

During our freshman year, I wrote a song called 3.16 about my misadventures on Valentine's Day of that year. Of course, the month and the date are wrong, but by the time I realized that, it was too late.

The song was in B Major and had a swing feel. As we had two trumpets in the band, we were able to make something that vaguely resembled Big Band arrangement, and people went crazy every time we played it, dancing up a storm.

My senior year, in 2005, I wrote Oh Marie as a sort of extension and rejection of 3.16. It was right before graduation, a moment that I also wrote Sarah Come Home and some songs that are so offensive that I can't in good conscience publish them.

But Oh Marie is a gem. It's pure joy, pure fluff, pure fun. There's no second degree. No irony. It's just a dance tune, plain and simple. Here's a Suno rendition of the chart, made from a vocal sketch I did in the early 2020s along with a MIDI version of the chart. It stays true to the chart and adds a warm crooning style that I can't do myself but is exactly what I dreamed of when I wrote the song.
